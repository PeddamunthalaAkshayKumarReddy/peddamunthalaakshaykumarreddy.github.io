Case study 1 · Manufacturing

AXON Manufacturing

Production, quality and delivery analytics for a garment-trims plant

One year of work orders from a plant that makes footwear, knitwear, printed fabric and woven labels for four fashion buyers, turned into a validated data model and three reporting layers.

Context
Team capstone, 7 members
Training dataset, 2026
My role
Presented the team's Excel dashboard and part of the Power BI report. Built the Tableau edition on my own.
Data
10,000 work orders
1 Jan - 31 Dec 2015
4 departments, 86 machines, 10 operations, 4 buyers
Tools
MySQL 8ExcelPower BITableau

Business problem

Plant managers had one flat export of 10,679 rows and no reporting layer. Output, quality and delivery couldn't be tracked in one place, or traced back to the machines, operations and buyers behind them.

  1. Q1Are we producing what buyers order, month by month?
  2. Q2Which machines, operations and departments drive rejected units?
  3. Q3Are orders delivered on time, and does it differ by buyer or department?
  4. Q4What is the recorded production value per unit, by operation and department?

StakeholdersPlant head, production planning, quality and maintenance, buyer account team

Solution

A cleaned star-schema model in MySQL feeding an Excel brief, a Power BI report and a Tableau workbook, with every headline KPI reconciled back to SQL before release.

  • Data model1 fact table and 8 dimensions with foreign keys, a clean reporting view and 15 KPI queries in MySQL 8.0
  • Power BI report6 pages and 4 tooltip pages, 48 DAX measures and a data governance page
  • Tableau workbook4 dashboards, 16 KPI cards, 24 charts, cross-filters and one-click reset
  • Data-quality logEvery fix and exception recorded with row counts

OutcomeAll four questions answered in a few clicks for any month, department, machine or operation, with figures that match the SQL source exactly.

How it was built

  1. Clean the sourceTeam

    Removed 679 blank rows from 10,679, logged two source exceptions and split the data into one fact table and eight dimensions.

  2. Model in MySQLTeam

    Star schema with eight foreign keys and a clean reporting view. Fifteen KPI queries ran on MySQL 8.0.

  3. Excel dashboardPresented

    KPI cards, charts and slicers for an executive summary, built on the cleaned data.

  4. Power BI reportPresented

    Six pages and four tooltip pages on an 11-table model with 48 DAX measures, plus a data governance page.

  5. Tableau editionBuilt by me

    Four dashboards with 16 KPI cards and 24 charts. Filters for month, department, machine and operation, and a one-click reset built with parameter actions.

  6. ReconcileBuilt by me

    Checked the Tableau KPIs against SQL for the full year and for six filtered slices before calling it done.

Key insights

30.2%Q2of rejected units come from two machines
Rejects follow volume, not weak machines

MC027 and MC026 account for 30.2% of the 491,023 rejected units, and the top ten machines for 65.8%. They are also the busiest machines. Across the 35 machines with 50 or more orders, rejection rates stay between 0.70% and 0.98%.

  • MC027 16.3%
  • MC026 13.9%
  • Next 8 machines 35.6%
  • Other 77 codes 34.2%
32.94%Q3of orders delivered on time
Late delivery is spread evenly

On-time, early and late each hold about a third of orders. On-time delivery ranges only from 31.74% (Nike) to 34.59% (Zara) across buyers, and from 31.93% (Footwear) to 33.55% (Knitwear) across departments.

100.05%Q1production efficiency (produced ÷ processed)
An efficiency above 100% pointed to a data rule

4,997 orders record more produced units than processed units, which lifts efficiency above 100%. I kept the source values and explained the rule in the dashboard footnote.

14.9MQ1units ordered but not recorded as processed
Processing falls a fifth short of orders

74.93M units were ordered and 60.02M processed, a recorded gap of 14,910,992 units. Machine utilization (processed ÷ ordered) is 80.10% for the year.

Recommendations

  1. Maintenance & quality
    Put inspection and preventive maintenance first on MC027 and MC026.

    They produce 30.2% of rejected units at normal rejection rates, so each point of improvement there saves the most units. Rank machines by rate as well as volume.

  2. Production planning
    Review how promised delivery dates are set.

    About a third of orders are late for every buyer and every department, which points to the scheduling rule rather than one account.

  3. Data owners
    Fix how produced and processed quantities are captured.

    4,997 orders show more produced than processed units. Efficiency shouldn't become a target until the rule is confirmed.

  4. Operations
    Track the ordered-to-processed gap every month, by operation.

    A 14.9M-unit gap (80.10% utilization) shows capacity running short; the monthly view shows where and when.

Case study 2 · Healthcare

AXON Healthcare

Clinical operations and revenue cycle analytics

Two years of electronic medical records across ten linked tables, turned into validated clinical and revenue-cycle dashboards with a clear record of what the data can and cannot support.

Context
Team capstone, 7 members
Training EMR dataset, 2026
My role
Built the Excel dashboard on a Power Pivot model, a nine-page Power BI report and a nine-dashboard Tableau workbook, and presented the key takeaways.
Data
10,000 patients and visits
1,000 doctors, 5 departments, 10 tables
22 Mar 2023 - 21 Mar 2025
Tools
Excel · Power PivotMySQLPower BITableau

Business problem

A multi-department hospital kept patients, doctors, visits, treatments, lab tests, billing, insurance policies and claims in ten separate tables. Leadership had no single view of care activity or the revenue cycle, and no way to tell which numbers the data could actually support.

  1. Q1How many visits are completed, cancelled or still scheduled, by department and month?
  2. Q2How is the workload spread across 1,000 doctors and five departments?
  3. Q3How much is billed, paid by patients and still outstanding?
  4. Q4How often are insurance claims approved or denied, and how long do they take?

StakeholdersHospital operations lead, department heads, billing and revenue-cycle team

Solution

Built clinical and revenue-cycle dashboards in Excel, Power BI and Tableau on ten tables stored in MySQL, and logged every data issue so no dashboard shows a KPI the data can't support.

  • Excel dashboardPower Pivot model with DAX measures, KPI cards, charts and slicers
  • Power BI report9 pages covering clinical operations, doctors, labs, billing, claims and policies
  • Tableau workbook9 dashboards, 36 KPI cards and 36 charts with filters and reset on every page
  • Data-issue logEvery data problem recorded with the rows affected and the action taken

OutcomeOne place to track visits, doctor workload, billing and claims, with plain notes wherever the data falls short.

  2. Excel dashboardBuilt by me

  3. Data in MySQLTeam

  4. Power BI reportBuilt by me

    Nine-page report with KPI cards, slicers and page navigation across clinical and revenue-cycle modules.

  5. Tableau workbookBuilt by me

    Nine dashboards with 36 charts and 36 KPI cards, filters, navigation and a reset control on every page.

Key insights

32.66%Q1of recorded visits completed
Visits split into three equal parts

Completed (3,266), cancelled (3,344) and scheduled (3,390) visits each make up about a third of the 10,000 visits. There are no appointment or check-in times, so waiting time and no-show rate cannot be measured.

42.98%Q3of billed charges recorded as patient-paid
The billing fields don't add up, so each is reported as recorded

Patients paid $10.92M of $25.41M billed, with $7.06M outstanding. In 4,042 bills, insurance covered + patient paid + outstanding differs from the billed amount by more than $0.02. The dashboards show each field as recorded and make no collection, profit or aging claims.

60.75%Q4of insurance claims fully approved
About one claim in eleven is denied

Of 10,000 claims, 6,075 were fully approved, 1,790 partly approved, 1,211 are pending and 924 were denied (9.24%). Processing takes 11.64 days on average across the 8,789 claims with both dates. Approved amounts are adjudicated values, not insurer cash.

80.35%Q2of patients carry a chronic condition
Chronic care dominates the patient base

8,035 of 10,000 patients have a chronic flag, split almost evenly across hypertension, diabetes, asthma and arthritis. Average stored age is 48.94, and 49.84% of visits are flagged for follow-up.

Recommendations

  1. Operations
    Report completion and cancellation together, and start capturing appointment and check-in times.

    A third of visits are cancelled, and without timestamps the hospital can't measure no-shows or waiting time.

  2. Billing team
    Agree billing field definitions before launching a collection-rate KPI.

    4,042 bills don't balance, so any collection rate built on them today would be misleading.

  3. Revenue cycle
    Compare provider approval and denial rates on the same denominator.

    With 9.24% of claims denied and 17.9% only partly approved, fair provider rankings need one consistent base.

  4. Clinical planning
    Plan follow-up capacity around chronic patients.

    80.35% of patients are chronic and half of all visits are flagged for follow-up, so repeat care drives the workload.
